Why I built Satoshi Mail

Satoshi Nakamoto's emails and forum posts are some of the most important writing in Bitcoin's history, but they're spread across archives, PDF exhibits and old mailing lists. I built Satoshi Mail so anyone can read them the way he would have: in one inbox, in order, with the replies around them. It was inspired by Luke Igel and Riley Walz's Jmail. It's open source, and every message links back to where it came from.

— Tyler Howell, founder (@TylerHowell88)

How Satoshi Mail is made

  • Messages are copied exactly, never rewritten. They come from the Satoshi Nakamoto Institute, nakamoto-archive, the Bitcoin Core repository, bitcoin.org, and correspondents' own copies filed as court evidence. Each one links to its original.
  • Everything is cross-checked against Blocktrainer's archive and satoshin.name. Dates are corrected only from a correspondent's own copy, and the date as originally sent is kept beside it.
  • Other people's privacy comes first. Private addresses, phone numbers and similar details of people who wrote to Satoshi are removed.
  • Satoshipedia is written with AI assistance, from the sources listed at the end of each article, and published by Tyler Howell. Quotations come from the archive. On Satoshi's identity it reports who has been named, by whom and how they responded, without taking a side.
  • Anyone can verify the archive. Every message has a SHA-256 fingerprint, and the list of fingerprints is timestamped in the Bitcoin blockchain (see Verify).
  • Corrections are welcome and are listed in the Update log.
  • Independent. No ads, no sponsors, no tracking cookies. Satoshi Mail isn't affiliated with Google, the Satoshi Nakamoto Institute or any other source.
