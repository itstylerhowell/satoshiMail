Bitcoin white paper

“Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System”

Title
Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System
Announced
31 October 2008
Working title
Electronic Cash Without a Trusted Third Party
Venue
Cryptography mailing list

The Bitcoin white paper, Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System, proposes “a purely peer-to-peer version of electronic cash” in which a peer-to-peer network prevents double-spending by timestamping transactions into a chain of proof-of-work.[1] Satoshi announced it on 31 October 2008.[2]

Drafts

In August 2008 Satoshi described the paper to Adam Back under the working title Electronic Cash Without a Trusted Third Party and offered a pre-release draft (the email). He asked Wei Dai for the date of b-money so he could cite it. nakamoto-archive keeps a version of the paper dated 3 October 2008.[3]

Announcement

Satoshi posted to the Cryptography mailing list: “I've been working on a new electronic cash system that's fully peer-to-peer, with no trusted third party.” He listed the main properties, starting with “Double-spending is prevented with a peer-to-peer network.” (the announcement). The first reply, from James A. Donald, questioned whether it could scale. The paper is hosted at bitcoin.org.[1]

References

The paper cites eight works, among them Wei Dai's b-money and Adam Back's Hashcash. It does not cite Nick Szabo's bit gold.[1] See Precursors of Bitcoin.
