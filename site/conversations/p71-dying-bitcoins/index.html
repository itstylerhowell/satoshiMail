Dying bitcoins

BitcoinTalk forum · 7 messages · from 21 June 2010 · with Gavin Andresen, Satoshi Nakamoto, laszlo, virtualcoin

Read in Satoshi Mail Archive source

virtualcoin · 21 June 2010, 13:22 UTC · original
Hello, if somebody's loosing his wallet (e.g. due to disk crash) he's not able to get back his coins, is he? So every time a person looses coins, they're lost forever? So the bitcoin network will slowly shrink over time? (Because there will always be people who loose wallets!) TIA virtualcoin edit: Forgot to mention the coins of users that are try out bitcoin and then never use it again.
laszlo · 21 June 2010, 13:54 UTC · original
They are lost for good.  Think of it like someone losing their wallet in the ocean.  It seems like it would be a problem but it's really not that significant.  People lose their gold jewelry all the time but it doesn't make much of a difference to the value of gold. I wonder though, is there a point where the difficulty of generating a new coinbase is so high that it would make more sense to try to recover keys for lost coins or steal other people's coins instead?  The difficulty of that is really high so for now it makes a lot more sense to generate but I just wonder what the real figures are.. would that ever become more productive?  Maybe Satoshi can address this..
virtualcoin · 21 June 2010, 14:33 UTC · original
Quote from: laszlo on June 21, 2010, 01:54:29 PM
They are lost for good.  Think of it like someone losing their wallet in the ocean.  It seems like it would be a problem but it's really not that significant.  People lose their gold jewelry all the time but it doesn't make much of a difference to the value of gold.
But loosing 1 bitcoin in the ocean of a maximum of 21.000.000 coins seems significant to me, so not comparable to gold and jewelry. So only 21000 losses of 1000 coins can happen. That's not much!
Gavin Andresen · 21 June 2010, 15:01 UTC · original
Quote from: virtualcoin on June 21, 2010, 02:33:31 PM
But loosing 1 bitcoin in the ocean of a maximum of 21.000.000 coins seems significant to me, so not comparable to gold and jewelry. So only 21000 losses of 1000 coins can happen. That's not much!
But remember that each bitcoins can be divided into 100 million pieces, as needed. And the more valuable they become, the more careful people will be not to lose them.
virtualcoin · 21 June 2010, 15:54 UTC · original
So how am I able to transfer less than BC0.01 without getting the message "error in amount" from the bitcoin program?
laszlo · 21 June 2010, 16:33 UTC · original
The current user interface doesn't support smaller than 0.01 increments but it is possible to support it in a future revision.
Satoshi Nakamoto · 21 June 2010, 17:48 UTC · original
Lost coins only make everyone else's coins worth slightly more.  Think of it as a donation to everyone.
Quote from: laszlo on June 21, 2010, 01:54:29 PM
I wonder though, is there a point where the difficulty of generating a new coinbase is so high that it would make more sense to try to recover keys for lost coins or steal other people's coins instead?  The difficulty of that is really high so for now it makes a lot more sense to generate but I just wonder what the real figures are.. would that ever become more productive?  Maybe Satoshi can address this..
Computers have to get about 2^200 times faster before that starts to be a problem.  Someone with lots of compute power could make more money by generating than by trying to steal.
